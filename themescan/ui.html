What is being bought and what is being sold, across every sector?context

We can't see actual fund flows, so each of 130+ themes (US sectors, countries, factors, commodities, bonds, currencies, thematic baskets) gets a flow score from two things we can see: how much it beat or lagged the world, and whether trading volume was mostly on up days (buying) or down days (selling). It doesn't change the Dashboard scores.

How to read this page (30 seconds)
  • Flow score (0–100) = half "return vs the world" + half "net buying" (share of dollar volume traded on up days minus down days), each ranked across all themes. 80+ strong buying, 60–80 buying, 40–60 neutral, 20–40 selling, under 20 heavy selling.
  • vs world: how far it beat (+) or lagged (−) the world index over the look-back.
  • Net buying: −1 to +1. Positive = buyers in control, negative = sellers.
  • Volume ×: trading volume vs its usual level. Above 1.5× with a strong score = heavy participation, not a quiet drift.
  • Turning: the last few days look very different from the whole window, e.g. a heavily sold theme that has started to recover, or a heavily bought one where buying is fading.

Limits: India sectors are not included (different benchmark and currency). This describes what already happened; tested over 133 themes since 2019, a high or low flow score did not predict the next 20–60 days (rank-correlation about 0, sign flips between periods). Use it to see where attention and money are moving, not to time trades.

Where is money going when AI sells off? (for every sector, see the Money flow tab)context

Money flows can't be observed directly, so this infers them: which themes beat what their AI exposure explains ("residual strength"), where dollar volume is net buying, and what followed past AI selloffs. Uses cached prices (weekdays only). It does not change the Dashboard scores.

How to read this page (30 seconds)

When the AI-linked themes fall, money has to go somewhere. We can't see money moving, so we look for themes that rose anyway:

  • Beat AI-link by (residual): how far the theme beat the world beyond what its sensitivity to AI explains. Big positive = a genuine winner, not just a rising tide. Main number.
  • Net buying: −1 to +1. Positive means more dollar volume traded on up days than down days (buyers in control).
  • AI link: how it moves with AI. −0.6 means when AI falls 1% it usually rises ~0.6% (a natural hedge). Near 0 = unrelated. Positive = falls with AI.
  • After past selloffs: in earlier AI selloffs, how much it beat the world over the next 60 days, and the share of times it did. Tells you whether the move tended to last.
  • Lenses passed (of 3): strong now + buyers in control + historically reliable. 3 of 3 is the strongest candidate.

Important: this shows where money went over the last 20 days. Tested on 13 past selloffs, today's winners did not reliably keep winning, so treat it as a map of what is happening, not a forecast.

How the page fits together

      
Dashboard is the product: ① prices, ② earnings and ③ news tone are the only things that change a theme's score. Each card shows how complete and how fresh that input is and has an Update button. When an update finishes, the table re-scans and a banner lists the themes whose verdict or conviction moved.

      
News, Rotation and Tools > Context tools add evidence you read alongside the score. Tools > Validation tests the method on history or records picks for a hindsight-free forward test. None of those change the Dashboard scores.

      
Reading the score

      
Conviction (0–100) = 40% price + 40% earnings + 20% news. Price and earnings were blended 50/50 first because earnings was the strongest signal; news was added at 20% because it improved results in 2024 onward and was neutral before, so it is the weakest-evidence component.

      
Strong ≥ 80 · Positive 65–80 · Neutral 35–65 · Avoid < 35. In tests, average forward return vs other themes rose with each tier on the global and India universes. Themes without usable earnings (e.g. crypto) are price-only and can't be Strong.

      
Evidence checklist (click a theme): every other source is checked but not scored. validated = backtested and in the score; live only = can't be backtested; tested: no edge = showed no predictive value, so context only.

      
How to use it: as a tilt. Avoid the Avoid themes and favour Strong / Positive ones across a diversified set. A concentrated top-5 rotation did not beat random picks on a broad universe; dropping the weakest themes did help (about +1 to +3% a year). Log picks weekly (Tools > Forward test) for a hindsight-free check. It describes what is already working; it does not predict the next bull run. Research screen, not investment advice.
